\noindent\textbf{Our Humble Response:} We sincerely thank the Associate Editor for this insightful guidance. We humbly acknowledge that in our original manuscript, the problem was introduced at a relatively high level, and the boundary between SRA and classical traffic engineering methods---such as Interior Gateway Protocol (IGP) dynamic weight optimization and static centrality-aware routing---was not articulated clearly enough. Following your advice, we have substantially revised the manuscript in four aspects:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em]
    \item \textbf{Explicit Research Gap and Structured 6-Column Taxonomy (Section~I, Table~I, and Section~II-B)}: We added a dedicated \textit{Research Gap} discussion in Section~I and expanded \textbf{Table~I} into a feature-by-feature comparison across five paradigms: \textit{Reactive Queue-Aware Routing}, \textit{IGP Link-Weight Optimization (Fortz \& Thorup [26])}, \textit{Open-Loop Static Centrality Routing [19]}, \textit{Data-Driven AI/DRL Routing [21, 22]}, and \textit{Proposed SRA}. We clarify that while reactive queue-based methods act only after physical buffer buildup (causing phase lag) and online IGP weight tuning requires full traffic matrices and induces severe route flapping ($98.3\%$ switching frequency when run online), SRA couples a continuous leaky-integrator structural stress state $S_i^{(k)}$ with minimum dwell-time block gating $T_d$ to proactively reshape routing costs without chattering.
    \item \textbf{Formal Mathematical Problem Formulation (Section~III-A, Eq.~(5))}: We formulated the resilient network governance task as a constrained discrete-time bi-criteria optimization problem (\textbf{Eq.~(5)}) that minimizes long-run peak generalized structural stress $\limsup_{k\to\infty}\max_{i\in V}\widetilde{\kappa}_i(S^{(k)}) + \eta_{\text{sw}}\bar{f}_{\text{sw}}$ subject to leaky-integrator state dynamics $S^{(k+1)}=(1-\alpha)S^{(k)}+\alpha q^{(k)}$, linear cost actuation $C^{(k)}=\mathbf{1}+\beta_I S^{(k)}$, and minimum dwell-time switching spacing $\tau_{m+1}-\tau_m \ge T_d$.
    \item \textbf{Direct Experimental Benchmarking Against Closely Related Methods (Section~IV-F, Table~4 / Table~IV, and Fig.~10)}: We implemented both \textit{Online IGP Weight Tuning (Fortz--Thorup)} and \textit{Static Betweenness Centrality Routing} alongside \textit{LIRP-D} and \textit{Dijkstra} across 30 independent random seeds on BA scale-free networks ($N=100, \rho=0.9$). As shown in \textbf{Table~4 (Table~IV)}, Full SRA achieves top-tier throughput ($89.76 \pm 0.76$ pkts/step, $99.65\%$ PDR) while cutting control-plane route switching frequency by \textbf{$79.7\%$} ($20.0\%$ vs.\ $98.3\%$ under online IGP weight tuning).
\end{enumerate}

We also added \textbf{Remark~2} to explicitly explain that $\Omega$ is a compact attracting set (which may contain bounded limit cycles near multi-path tie-breaking boundaries) rather than a single static point equilibrium, and that unique fixed-point convergence holds conditionally within a stable routing stratum (\textbf{Corollary~1}).

    \item \textbf{Non-Circular Peak-Shaving Certificate (Lemma~2 and Supplement Section~S-I)}: We replaced the circular conservation argument with an explicit \textbf{Route-Redistribution Budget} $B_j^{(m)} := (\kappa_j^{(m+1)}-\kappa_j^{(m)})_+$ and \textbf{Non-Overloading Diversion Condition} ($\kappa_j^{(m)} + B_j^{(m)} \le \kappa_{\max}^{(m)} - \delta$ for all $j \neq u$), proving a rigorous sufficient condition under which path switching strictly reduces global peak stress ($\kappa_{\max}^{(m+1)} \le \kappa_{\max}^{(m)} - \delta$).
    \item \textbf{Rigorous Capacity Bridge (Proposition~3 and Supplement Section~S-J)}: We corrected the reversed inequality to $\rho_{\max}(\kappa) = \min_{i\in V}(\mu_i/\kappa_i) \ge \mu_{\min}/\kappa_{\max} =: L(\kappa)$ and proved three exact results: (i) unconditional expansion of the worst-case guaranteed capacity lower bound $L(\kappa') > L(\kappa)$; (ii) exact inverse proportionality $\rho_{\max}(\kappa')/\rho_{\max}(\kappa) = \kappa_{\max}/\kappa'_{\max} > 1$ under homogeneous capacities ($\mu_i \equiv \mu$) or $\rho_{\max}(\widetilde{\kappa}')/\rho_{\max}(\widetilde{\kappa}) = \widetilde{\kappa}_{\max}/\widetilde{\kappa}'_{\max} > 1$ under generalized capacity-normalized stress $\widetilde{\kappa}_i(S;W,\mu)$; and (iii) the exact joint (\texttt{AND}) sufficient service-rate slack condition ($\mu_u/(\kappa_{\max}-d)\ge\rho_0$ and $\mu_j/(\kappa_j+B_j)\ge\rho_0,\ \forall j\neq u$) under heterogeneous capacities with unweighted centrality.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Our Humble Response:} We thank the Reviewer for highlighting this omission. In the revised \textbf{Section~III-B (Algorithm~1)} and \textbf{Section~IV-H}, we added an explicit time and space complexity analysis:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.8em]
    \item \textbf{Time Complexity}: At active routing recomputation epochs ($k \bmod T_d = 0$), computing all-pairs shortest paths and node centrality via Brandes' algorithm on a positively weighted graph requires $O(N(E + N\log N))$ time, followed by $O(N)$ state and cost updates. During the $T_d - 1$ frozen cooling steps within each dwell block, shortest-path trees and stress vectors are cached and reused, requiring only $O(N)$ vector arithmetic per step. Hence, the amortized per-step control-plane time complexity is $O\!\left(\frac{N(E + N\log N)}{T_d} + N\right)$.
    \item \textbf{Space Complexity}: Working memory for Brandes' traversal and state vectors is $O(N + E)$ ($O(N)$ for node states/costs and $O(E)$ for adjacency lists), and storing all-pairs next-hop routing tables takes $O(N^2)$ space.
\end{itemize}
