\cbox{Associate Editor --- Comment AE.1 (Establishing Technical Novelty, Problem Formulation, and Systematic Comparison)}{The Associate Editor advises that the technical novelty of SRA should be more clearly established by explicitly distinguishing it from existing adaptive routing and load-balancing approaches---especially those based on dynamic link weights and centrality optimization---and by providing a clearer mathematical problem formulation along with a systematic comparison against closely related methods.}

\noindent\textbf{Response:} We thank the Associate Editor for this constructive guidance. We agree that in the original manuscript, the problem was introduced at a high level, and the boundary between SRA and classical traffic engineering methods---such as Interior Gateway Protocol (IGP) dynamic weight optimization and static centrality-aware routing---was not articulated clearly enough. Following your suggestion, we have revised the manuscript in four aspects:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em]
    \item \textbf{Explicit Research Gap and Structured 6-Column Taxonomy (Section~1 / Section~I, Table~1 / Table~I, and Section~2.2 / Section~II-B)}: We added a dedicated \textit{Explicit Research Gap} discussion in \textbf{Section~1 (Section~I)} and expanded \textbf{Table~1 (Table~I)} into a 6-column comparison across five paradigms (\textit{Reactive Queue-Aware Routing}, \textit{Dynamic IGP Weight Tuning (Fortz \& Thorup [2])}, \textit{Static Centrality Optimization [3, 7]}, \textit{AI / DRL-Based Routing [4, 8]}, and \textit{SRA (This Work)}) evaluated across seven dimensions: \textit{Sensing Signal}, \textit{Control Loop \& Memory}, \textit{Anti-Chattering}, \textit{Stability Proof}, \textit{Hetero.\ Demand/Cap.}, \textit{Per-Update Complexity}, and \textit{Interpretability}. We clarify that while reactive queue-based schemes suffer phase lag and memoryless online IGP weight tuning induces severe route flapping ($99.5\%$ switching rate, $100.0\%$ steady-state tail), SRA couples a continuous leaky-integrator structural stress state $S_i^{(k)}$ with minimum dwell-time gating $T_d$ to proactively reshape routing costs without chattering.
    \item \textbf{Formal Mathematical Problem Formulation (Section~3.1 / Section~III-A, Eq.~(4))}: We formulated the resilient network governance task as a constrained discrete-time bi-criteria optimization problem (\textbf{Eq.~(4)}) that minimizes long-run peak generalized structural stress $\limsup_{k\to\infty}\max_{i\in V}\widetilde{\kappa}_i(S^{(k)}) + \eta_{\text{sw}}\bar{f}_{\text{sw}}$ subject to leaky-integrator state dynamics $S^{(k+1)}=(1-\alpha)S^{(k)}+\alpha q^{(k)}$, linear cost actuation $C^{(k)}=\mathbf{1}+\beta_I S^{(k)}$, and minimum dwell-time switching spacing $\tau_{m+1}-\tau_m \ge T_d$.
    \item \textbf{Direct Experimental Benchmarking Against Closely Related Methods (Section~4.6 / Section~IV-F, Table~4 / Table~IV, and Fig.~10)}: We implemented \textit{IGP Weight Tuning (Memoryless Fortz--Thorup)}, \textit{IGP Damped FT (EWMA, $T_d=1$)}, and \textit{Static BC Routing (One-Shot Open-Loop)} alongside \textit{LIRP-D} and \textit{Dijkstra} across 30 independent random seeds on BA scale-free networks ($N=100, \rho=0.9, K_{\max}=200, T=1{,}000$). As shown in \textbf{Table~4 (Table~IV)}, Full SRA achieves $89.72 \pm 0.72$ pkts/step ($99.63 \pm 0.72\%$ PDR, $+9.64\%$ throughput over Memoryless IGP Weight Tuning at $p_{\text{Holm}}=1.39 \times 10^{-5}$) while slashing control-plane route switching frequency by \textbf{$80.4\%$} ($19.5\%$ vs.\ $99.5\%$ under both Memoryless IGP and Damped IGP FT; $20.0\%$ vs.\ $100.0\%$ in the steady-state tail, $p_{\text{Holm}}=3.46 \times 10^{-7}$).
\end{enumerate}

\cbox{Associate Editor --- Comment AE.2 (Theoretical Rigor in Theorems 1 \& 2, Lemma 2, and Proposition 3)}{The Associate Editor recommends carefully re-examining the convergence claims in Theorems 1 and 2---particularly the distinction between attraction to a limit set and convergence to a unique equilibrium---and providing stronger mathematical support for the relationship between structural stress reduction and network capacity improvement in Lemma 2 and Proposition 3.}

\noindent\textbf{Response:} We thank the Associate Editor for highlighting these important theoretical questions. Upon careful re-examination, we agree that our original phrasing in Theorem~1 conflated global set attraction with convergence to a unique fixed point, and that the proofs of Theorem~2, Lemma~2, and Proposition~3 required stricter derivations (including correcting a reversed minimum inequality in the original Appendix~J). We have thoroughly revised \textbf{Sections~3.3, 3.4, 3.5, and 3.6 (Sections~III-D, III-E, III-F, and III-G)} as well as \textbf{Supplementary Material Sections~S-A, S-B, S-C, S-D, S-I, and S-J}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em]
    \item \textbf{Distinction Between Limit Set Attraction ($\Omega$) and Unique Point Equilibrium (Theorem~1, Remark~2, and Supplement Sections~S-A--S-C)}: We first completed the coordinate-wise box-invariance proof in \textbf{Lemma~1 (Supplement Section~S-A)} by explicitly defining $\delta^+_{k,i} := \max\{S_i^{(k)}-1, 0\}$ and $\delta^-_{k,i} := \max\{-S_i^{(k)}, 0\}$. Next, using the Krasovskii regularization $\mathcal{N}(S) := \bigcap_{\delta>0}\overline{\mathrm{co}}(\mathrm{norm}(\kappa(B_\delta(S))))$ and Aubin--Cellina upper-semicontinuous set-valued map theory [39], we revised \textbf{Theorem~1} to establish global attraction on $X=[0,1]^N$ to the non-empty, compact, positively invariant limit set $\Omega = \bigcap_{n\ge 0} F^n(X) \subset X$ ($\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathrm{dist}(S^{(k)}, \Omega)=0$). We also added \textbf{Remark~2} to explicitly explain that $\Omega$ is a compact attracting set (which may contain bounded limit cycles near multi-path tie-breaking boundaries) rather than a single static point equilibrium, and that unique fixed-point convergence holds conditionally within a stable routing stratum (\textbf{Corollary~1}).
    \item \textbf{Exact Discrete-Time Drop-Jump Energy Bound (Theorem~2 and Supplement Section~S-D)}: We removed the heuristic continuous-time analogy and derived an exact discrete-time one-step difference inequality for the implemented normalized feedback $q^{(k)} = \mathrm{norm}(\kappa^{(k)}) \in [0,1]^N$ and tracking energy $E_k = \|S^{(k)}-q^{(k)}\|_2^2$, proving via Weighted Young's inequality ($\lambda = \alpha/(1-\alpha)$) that $E_{k+1} \le (1-\alpha)E_k + \frac{1}{\alpha}\|q^{(k)}-q^{(k+1)}\|_2^2 \le (1-\alpha)E_k + \bar{\Delta}^2/\alpha$ and $\limsup_{k\to\infty} E_k \le \bar{\Delta}^2/\alpha^2$.
    \item \textbf{Non-Circular Peak-Shaving Certificate (Lemma~2 and Supplement Section~S-I)}: We replaced the circular conservation argument with an explicit \textbf{Route-Redistribution Budget} $B_j^{(m)} := (\kappa_j^{(m+1)}-\kappa_j^{(m)})_+$ and \textbf{Non-Overloading Diversion Condition} ($\kappa_j^{(m)} + B_j^{(m)} \le \kappa_{\max}^{(m)} - \delta$ for all $j \neq u$), proving a rigorous sufficient condition under which path switching strictly reduces global peak stress ($\kappa_{\max}^{(m+1)} \le \kappa_{\max}^{(m)} - \delta$).
    \item \textbf{Rigorous Capacity Bridge (Proposition~3 and Supplement Section~S-J)}: We corrected the reversed inequality to $\rho_{\max}(\kappa) = \min_{i\in V}(\mu_i/\kappa_i) \ge \mu_{\min}/\kappa_{\max} =: L(\kappa)$ and proved three exact results: (i) unconditional expansion of the worst-case guaranteed capacity lower bound $L(\kappa') > L(\kappa)$; (ii) exact inverse proportionality $\rho_{\max}(\kappa')/\rho_{\max}(\kappa) = \kappa_{\max}/\kappa'_{\max} > 1$ under homogeneous capacities ($\mu_i \equiv \mu$) or $\rho_{\max}(\widetilde{\kappa}')/\rho_{\max}(\widetilde{\kappa}) = \widetilde{\kappa}_{\max}/\widetilde{\kappa}'_{\max} > 1$ under generalized capacity-normalized stress $\widetilde{\kappa}_i(S;W,\mu)$; and (iii) the exact joint (\texttt{AND}) sufficient service-rate slack condition ($\mu_u/(\kappa_{\max}-d)\ge\rho_0$ and $\mu_j/(\kappa_j+B_j)\ge\rho_0,\ \forall j\neq u$) under heterogeneous capacities with unweighted centrality.
\end{enumerate}

\end{itemize}

\cbox{Reviewer 1 --- Comment R1.8 (Add Controlled Noise Injection Experiments)}{The Reviewer notes that performance sensitivity to control-plane signal degradation was not explored, and suggests adding controlled noise injection experiments to validate practical reliability.}

\noindent\textbf{Response:} We thank the Reviewer for this insightful suggestion.
